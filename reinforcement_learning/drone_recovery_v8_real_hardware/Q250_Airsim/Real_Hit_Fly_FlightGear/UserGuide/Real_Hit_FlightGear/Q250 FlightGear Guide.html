1The big picture

Think of it as a film set In the AirSim story the aircraft lived in its own room. Here it does not. FlightGear is only the TV screen: it draws a drone and flies nothing. The real flying is done inside the brain room, where ArduPilot runs its own physics for a pretend drone described by one file, q250.json. The same program is the brain and the body, so the "aircraft" is a small text file.

Room 1 · FlightGear (Windows)

The TV. It listens on UDP port 5503 and draws whatever position and angles it is sent. It never decides how the drone moves. Started by FlightGear_quad.bat.

Room 2 · SITL (WSL)

Body and brain. ArduPilot's own physics moves the pretend drone from q250.json, and the same ArduPilot code that runs on the real flight controller steers it, with q250_throw.parm. Folder ~/sitl_q250_throw.

Room 3 · Pilot (PowerShell)

Identical to the AirSim story: hit_real.py is the examiner, radio_pilot.py turns your Bumblebee radio into stick commands.

The rule that is the mirror of Part 1 In AirSim the body is settings.json and q250.json is never read. In FlightGear it is the other way round: the body is q250.json, and settings.json plays no part. Same real drone, two descriptions, two languages.

Analogy: figure skater Inertia is how hard it is to spin you. Arms tucked in you spin easily; arms out, slowly. A drone with its motors far out on long arms is "arms out". The estimate from box + motors is 0.00124 / 0.00139 / 0.00229 kg·m², and the file uses twice that: with the real gains, 1× made the simulated drone shake, 2× was steady. The estimate leaves out plates, arms, Pi, GPS mast and wires.

hoverThrOut is tuned by flying, not by formula The starting guess is E × 1.107 (0.149 → 0.165). Your book records that 0.149 gave a hover reading of 13 % and 0.165 gave 14 %, against 14.9 % real. The readout is whole numbers, so 14 is as close as it can show. If the reading is off, move hoverThrOut by about 0.016 per 1 % missing, copy the file in again, and repeat the hover check.

2.5  EXPO and idle: here they are in play

In AirSim we set EXPO to 0 and idle to 0. In FlightGear it is the opposite: the pretend motor is deliberately curved, because that is what real motors do.

Analogy: volume knob and amplifier A real motor reacts unevenly: the first half of the throttle does little, the last half does a lot. ArduPilot adds a matching bend in the knob (MOT_THST_EXPO 0.49) so the sound grows evenly. In q250.json, propExpo 0.49 is the uneven amplifier. The two bends cancel, so thrust is (about) proportional to what ArduPilot asks for. Same idea as the real drone. Change only one of the two and the match is gone.
 Real droneFlightGearAirSim
MOT_THST_EXPO0.490.490
MOT_SPIN_MIN0.150.150
MOT_SPIN_MAX0.950.951
Motor curve lives inMotors and propsq250.jsonAirSim (a straight line)

Do not carry this pair over to AirSim Expo 0.49 plus idle 0.15 are correct here because q250.json supplies the matching curved motor. Copied into the AirSim folder they over-drove the motors and the test drone climbed to about 95 m (Part 1, chapter 2).

The battery, and why the pilot script sets 15.3 V

On start radio_pilot.py sets SIM_BATT_VOLTAGE 15.3. That is a realistic loaded 4S voltage. Your notes from 12 Nov explain why it matters: the simulator's default was a 3S pack at 12.6 V, which made the motors weak enough that the height hold sank. Every FlightGear hit run since then is at 15.3 V (the volt column). The same value also lets the voltage check pass (minimum 14.4 V).

5Fly the hits with your own radio

Analogy: a skater who gets shoved You skate steadily (hover). The examiner shoves you hard (hit). You flail and catch your balance (PPO levels the drone). You stop yourself falling (height-hold brakes). Then you glide back to the spot where you were pushed (return).

This part is the same program and the same radio as in AirSim. The only thing that changes is what is on the other end of the cable.

F3

Take off yourself and hover at 10 to 11.5 m

  1. Throttle stick low, arm with CH9.
  2. Take off in AltHold (CH8) and climb to 10 to 11.5 m. The examiner refuses hits below 8 m.
  3. Switch CH8 to PosHold, all sticks in the middle.

The status line should show: height at least 8 m, POSHOLD, throttle OK, GPS fix, within 5 m of the start point.

The strict throttle-middle rule Before a hit the throttle must read 1400 to 1600. Outside it: REFUSED: throttle stick middle. This is ArduPilot's own throttle dead zone (THR_DZ, default 100). In PosHold the drone holds height only while the stick is inside it. Outside it, the drone would obey the stick instead of holding height when the examiner hands control back.
F4

Do the hits

  1. Right button: choose the size. It stays until you press another.
  2. One left button: flip, bird or spin. The checks run.
  3. Hands off the sticks. Watch FlightGear, and read the console.
  4. Wait for back to you in POSHOLD and HIT OK. Then the next one.

Checks on every hit: armed and in the air, in POSHOLD or LOITER, height ≥ 8 m, throttle centred, 3D GPS fix with ≥ 5 satellites and healthy EKF, voltage ≥ 14.4 V, within 5 m of the start point. Safety nets: the hit is cut if the drone would fall below 5 m while braking; below 3 m it rescues with full thrust and that hit counts as failed. None happened on 18 Nov.

6.4  How to read it

Spins are easy

The drone stays flat. Height lost 0 to 0.04 m, drift 0.1 to 0.23 m. Recovery takes the longest (1.15 to 1.29 s) only because it must stop a spin.

Flips and birds cost height

Upside down means thrust points down. Easy flip 3.98 m, medium flips 3.09 to 3.45 m, hard flip 6.17 m. Birds: 1.5 m easy and medium, 4.57 m hard.

One drift stands out

Hit 4 (flip, medium) slid 8.73 m. The two other medium flips slid 0.97 m and 2.39 m. All three came back to within 0.11 m. One run is not a trend, but your field is only about 10 m wide.

Return takes most of the time

The fall is over in about 1 to 2 s. Flying back takes 2.0 to 9.5 s, longest after the flips that slid furthest.

8What this proves, and what it does not

Shown by this run

  • The whole chain works: radio → pilot → brain → ArduPilot physics → examiner → PPO → hand-back.
  • The PPO (trained on the F450, never retrained) recovers a 5-inch body in all 12 hits.
  • The doorman checks refuse correctly, and no rescue was needed.
  • Recovery plus return works, back within 0.17 m.

Not shown

  • Real motors: lag, ESC behaviour, prop damage, vibration.
  • Wind (--wind=0@0, and FlightGear never moves the drone anyway), GPS jitter.
  • Hits from higher or lower than about 11 m.
  • That the timing check was passed that day (not in your files).
  • Why AirSim loses more height (chapter 7).

Also worth knowing: the real drone's hover has since drifted to ≈ 0.137 in the logs of 11 to 14 Nov, while the simulator learns 0.158 to 0.163. The hits ran at one fixed start height, and your run of 17 Nov exists in your files but is not used here (it ends with four hits you took over; tell me if you want it described). FlightGear is only the picture: the same numbers come out with FlightGear closed, as long as the timing check passes.
