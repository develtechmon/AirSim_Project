What happens after a hit now

The "5 m" was never a fence. FIELD_R_M is only checked at the moment you press a hit button: if the drone is more than 5 m from where you started the script, the hit is refused and the drone just keeps hovering in your POSHOLD. It never moved the drone, never did RTL, never landed. Before this version, after a hit the drone simply stayed wherever it had slid to — lower, or 5–8 m higher after a rescue, and metres away — in this simulator up to 48 m away after a flip.

Why the arrow is the whole story

A quadcopter can only push one way: straight out of its own belly. It cannot push sideways, and it cannot pull. So wherever the drone is pointing, that is where the push goes.

Level — the arrow points down

All of the push fights gravity. The drone holds height and stays put. That's every spin: it turns on the spot and never tips past about 20°, so it never stops being a helicopter.

Upside down — the arrow points at the ground

Bird and flip both tip the drone past 130°. Now the props push it toward the ground and sideways. It falls faster than gravity alone and builds up speed across the sky.

The PPO turns it upright again in about half a second (0.2–1.2 s in this run), at every level. But by then the drone already has the speed. The height hold and ArduPilot then have to brake it — and most of the drift happens during that braking, after the drone is already level.

Every hit, the numbers

Same words as the Hit n | ... line hit_real.py prints after each hit. drift is how far it got from the hit point; back is how long the fly-back took and how close it stopped. How every hit ended

Every flight is here, not just one. Tap a ▶ button to watch that exact hit in the replay above. Dot colour: green = PPO recovered cleanly, orange = PPO recovered but dropped below 5 m or slid more than 5 m, red = rescue took over.

RESCUED means the drone was falling fast enough to pass 3 m, so the full-thrust rescue took over — the safety net working. The rescue makes it climb 5–8 m; the fly-back then brings it down to the hit height.

What the fly-back does not fix: it can only start once the drone is level again, so during the hit and the recovery the drone still slides — a few metres for a spin, much more for bird and flip in this simulator. It comes back afterwards, but for those seconds it is wherever the slide took it. Spin first; bird and flip only when the space around the field is clear.

What I can't tell you

The simulator has perfectly still air and perfect GPS. On the real drone, wind will push the fly-back and GPS noise of about a metre means "back" will be within a metre or two, not 0.1 m — that is why the script counts anything inside 1 m as back, and hands it to you anyway after 30 s. Three repeats per cell shows the pattern, not exact odds.
