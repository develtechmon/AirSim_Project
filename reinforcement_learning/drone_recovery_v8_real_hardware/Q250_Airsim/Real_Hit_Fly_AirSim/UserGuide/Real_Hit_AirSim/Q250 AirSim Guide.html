1The big picture

Think of it as a flight school There is the aircraft (what the body does when you push it), the autopilot brain (the same ArduPilot code that runs on the real flight controller), and the student with an examiner (your radio plus the hit test). In the simulator each one lives in its own room, and the rooms talk through cables made of network ports.

Room 1 · AirSim (Windows)

The aircraft. It reads settings.json to know the weight, arm length, motor strength and body box, and it moves the drone with its own physics.

Room 2 · SITL (WSL)

The autopilot brain. Real ArduPilot code, started from the folder ~/sitl_q250. It reads q250_sim.parm (the gains and hover value of your real drone).

Room 3 · Pilot (PowerShell)

hit_real.py is the examiner: it hits the drone and runs the PPO recovery. radio_pilot.py turns your Bumblebee radio into stick commands, so you fly with your own hands.

The one rule that caused most of our confusion AirSim does not read q250.json. That file is FlightGear's body (ArduPilot's built-in physics loads it with --model quad:@ROMFS/models/q250.json). In AirSim the body is only settings.json. So the same real drone is described twice, in two different languages: q250.json for FlightGear, settings.json for AirSim.
 FlightGear runAirSim run (this guide)

Why bother with two simulators? Two different bodies can recover from the same hit only if the recovery logic is sound. One simulator alone could be luck of its own physics.

2Where every number comes from

Analogy: a tailor To make a suit that fits, the tailor measures you first (the real drone), writes the measurements on a card (settings.json = the body), and a second card says how you move and react (q250_sim.parm = the brain's habits). Nothing on either card is guessed from nowhere.

Analogy: figure skater Inertia is how hard it is to spin you. Arms tucked in, you spin fast and easily; arms out, slowly. A drone with heavy motors far out on long arms is "arms out": harder to start and harder to stop. AirSim works inertia out from the box plus four weights at the arm tips.
Show the inertia working (and why MOTOR_KG is 0.070, not 0.033)

Box mass = 0.5445 − 4 × 0.070 = 0.2645 kg. Each motor sits 0.125 × 0.707 m from the roll and pitch axes, and 0.125 m from the yaw axis.

The finding behind the "2×": with the real drone's gains, an inertia of 1× made the simulated drone shake, 2× was steady. The estimate leaves out plates, arms, Pi, GPS mast and wires, so the real drone is plausibly heavier to spin than the pure parts suggest. Honest note: pitch inertia in AirSim ends up about 14 % under the FlightGear value; roll 7 % and yaw 2 % under.

Theory is not the final word on thrust weight ÷ (4 × hover) gives 8.96 N for 0.149. Your file uses 8.1, which you tuned by flying: the AirSim hover reading must land on the real one. Rule from your build book: new = old × measured hover % ÷ (E × 100). With 8.1, AirSim read 15 at 10 m, the real drone reads ≈14. The simple model predicts 16.5, so there is a small ≈10 % gap I have not isolated; I left the number alone because the reading matches the real one.

2.5  The EXPO gap: real 0.49, AirSim 0

You noticed that the real drone and FlightGear use MOT_THST_EXPO 0.49 and AirSim uses 0, and that FlightGear also has idle (0.15). Here is why that is correct, and where it stops being.

Analogy: volume knob and amplifier Some amplifiers react unevenly: the first half of the knob does little and the last half does a lot. A good designer adds a matching curve in the knob so the sound grows evenly. EXPO is that matching curve. The real motor is uneven, so ArduPilot bends its command with 0.49. In AirSim the motor is perfectly even (thrust = THRUST_N × command), so it needs no bend. Bending an even amplifier anyway makes it uneven.
 Real droneFlightGearAirSim
MOT_THST_EXPO0.490.490
MOT_SPIN_MIN0.150.150
MOT_SPIN_MAX0.950.951
MOT_SPIN_ARM0.10.10
Where the motor curve livesIn the motors and propsIn q250.json (propExpo 0.49, spin 0.15 / 0.95)In AirSim: a straight line
Result for the controllerThrust is (about) proportional to what ArduPilot asks for, in all three
What we learned the hard way Copying the FlightGear values (expo 0.49, idle 0.15) into AirSim over-drives the motors by about 2×, and idle ≈ hover. The test drone then shot up to about 95 m. Never copy the throw file into the AirSim folder, and never copy battery-voltage lines into AirSim.

Honest limits of "they cancel"

The match is about thrust per command. It does not make AirSim's motors behave like real ones in other ways: motor lag (AirSim uses a simple delay), voltage sag, propeller inflow loss, drag and yaw torque all differ. A true curve of your real motors needs a thrust-stand test, which we have not done. To force expo into AirSim itself you would change AirSim's C++ (ArduCopterApi.hpp, which I do not have here) and rebuild, and because ArduPilot's expo would then cancel it again, the thrust-per-command would stay the same. Little to gain.

Analogy: a shop-bought game console The ready-made AirSim only knows one drone (the F450, called "Generic"). We build AirSim from its recipe (source code), teach it a second drone, and bake a new double-click program. You do this once. After that, switching drones is one settings file.

#ifndef msr_airlib_vehicles_ArduCopter_hpp
#define msr_airlib_vehicles_ArduCopter_hpp

#include "vehicles/multirotor/firmwares/arducopter/ArduCopterApi.hpp"
#include "vehicles/multirotor/MultiRotorParams.hpp"
#include "common/AirSimSettings.hpp"
#include "sensors/SensorFactory.hpp"
#include <cmath>
#include <string>

namespace msr
{
namespace airlib
{

    class ArduCopterParams : public MultiRotorParams
    {
    public:
        ArduCopterParams(const AirSimSettings::MavLinkVehicleSetting& vehicle_setting, std::shared_ptr<const SensorFactory> sensor_factory)
            : sensor_factory_(sensor_factory)
        {
            connection_info_ = getConnectionInfo(vehicle_setting);
        }

        virtual ~ArduCopterParams() = default;

        virtual std::unique_ptr<MultirotorApiBase> createMultirotorApi() override
        {
            return std::unique_ptr<MultirotorApiBase>(new ArduCopterApi(this, connection_info_));
        }

    protected:
        virtual void setupParams() override
        {
            auto& params = getParams();

            // "Model" in settings.json selects the drone (default model name is "Generic")
            if (connection_info_.model == "Q250") {
                setupFrameQ250(params); // 5-inch drone, numbers from settings.json "Parameters"
            }
            else {
                setupFrameGenericQuad(params); // F450, unchanged (all Stage 1-3 training)
            }
        }

        // 5-inch drone. Same structure as setupFrameGenericQuad() in MultiRotorParams.hpp,
        // but every number comes from settings.json "Parameters" (or the default below).
        void setupFrameQ250(Params& params)
        {
            params.rotor_count = 4;
            std::vector<real_T> arm_lengths(params.rotor_count, q250Value("Q250_ARM_M", 0.125f));

            params.mass = q250Value("Q250_MASS_KG", 0.55f);
            real_T motor_assembly_weight = q250Value("Q250_MOTOR_KG", 0.033f);
            real_T box_mass = params.mass - params.rotor_count * motor_assembly_weight;

            // rotor: prop size, motor delay, and max thrust of one motor
            params.rotor_params.propeller_diameter = q250Value("Q250_PROP_D_M", 0.127f);
            params.rotor_params.control_signal_filter_tc = q250Value("Q250_MOTOR_TC_S", 0.02f);
            // choose max_rpm so that calculateMaxThrust() gives exactly Q250_THRUST_N
            // (thrust = C_T * air_density * (rpm/60)^2 * D^4, AirSim's own formula in RotorParams.hpp)
            const real_T thrust_n = q250Value("Q250_THRUST_N", 5.4f);
            const real_T d4 = static_cast<real_T>(std::pow(params.rotor_params.propeller_diameter, 4));
            const real_T rev_per_s = std::sqrt(thrust_n / (params.rotor_params.C_T * params.rotor_params.air_density * d4));
            params.rotor_params.max_rpm = rev_per_s * 60.0f;
            params.rotor_params.calculateMaxThrust();

            // centre block (stack + battery + Pi) as a box
            params.body_box.x() = q250Value("Q250_BODY_X_M", 0.15f);
            params.body_box.y() = q250Value("Q250_BODY_Y_M", 0.05f);
            params.body_box.z() = q250Value("Q250_BODY_Z_M", 0.07f);
            real_T rotor_z = 2.5f / 100; // same as the F450

            initializeRotorQuadX(params.rotor_poses, params.rotor_count, arm_lengths.data(), rotor_z);
            computeInertiaMatrix(params.inertia, params.body_box, params.rotor_poses, box_mass, motor_assembly_weight);
        }

        // value from settings.json "Parameters", or the default if the key is missing
        real_T q250Value(const std::string& key, real_T default_value) const
        {
            auto it = connection_info_.params.find(key);
            return (it != connection_info_.params.end()) ? static_cast<real_T>(it->second) : default_value;
        }

        virtual const SensorFactory* getSensorFactory() const override
        {
            return sensor_factory_.get();
        }

        static const AirSimSettings::MavLinkConnectionInfo& getConnectionInfo(const AirSimSettings::MavLinkVehicleSetting& vehicle_setting)
        {
            return vehicle_setting.connection_info;
        }

    private:
        AirSimSettings::MavLinkConnectionInfo connection_info_;
        std::shared_ptr<const SensorFactory> sensor_factory_;
    };
}

What "throttle 15" means: it is not the battery and not the PWM. It is the controller's thrust demand in percent, the same scale as MOT_THST_HOVER (0.149 = 15 %). So 15 means "the controller needs 15 % thrust to hold still". Then land with mode land.

5Fly the hits with your own radio

Analogy: a skater who gets shoved You skate steadily (hover). The examiner shoves you hard (hit). You flail and catch your balance (PPO levels the drone). You stop yourself from falling (height-hold brakes). Then you glide back to the spot you were pushed from (return). Every phase has a name in the data.
F1

Open the examiner (hit_real.py)

Where: a Windows PowerShell with your (airsimcode) environment, in the project folder:

cd C:\Users\lukas\Desktop\My_Project\AirSim_Project\reinforcement_learning\drone_recovery_v8_real_hardware\Q250_Airsim\Real_Hit_Fly_FlightGear
py .\hit_real.py --conn tcp:127.0.0.1:5762 --baud 115200

It connects to the brain's second port (5762) and waits, printing the height. --conn tcp:127.0.0.1:5762 --baud 115200 is the only difference from the real drone's command: on the real drone the Pi talks to the flight controller over a serial wire; here it talks over the network.

F2

Open the pilot (radio_pilot.py)

Where: a second PowerShell window, same folder, same environment.

py .\radio_pilot.py fly

It reads your Bumblebee radio (joystick) and sends your sticks to the brain (port 5763). On start it also writes a few settings the test needs (flight modes, RC9_OPTION 154, MOT_SPIN_ARM 0, SIM_BATT_VOLTAGE 15.3 and others).

F3

Take off yourself and hover

  1. Arm with CH9, climb with CH3, switch CH8 to POSHOLD.
  2. Climb above 8 m: the examiner refuses lower hits. Today's runs were at about 24 m and later 40 m.
  3. Bring all sticks to the middle. The throttle stick must sit in the centre band (see the box below).
The strict throttle-middle rule Before a hit the throttle must read 1400 to 1600. Outside it, the hit is refused: REFUSED: throttle stick middle (CH3 1632). This is not a bug. It is ArduPilot's own throttle dead zone: in PosHold the drone holds height only while the stick is inside it (parameter THR_DZ, default 100). Outside it, the drone would obey the stick instead of holding height when the examiner hands control back.
F4

One hit

  1. Right button: choose the size (easy, medium or hard). It stays until you press another.
  2. One left button: flip, bird or spin. The checks run.
  3. Hands off the sticks. Read the console, there are no beeps in the simulator.
  4. Wait for back to you in POSHOLD. Then the next hit.

Checks done on every hit: armed and in the air, in POSHOLD or LOITER, height ≥ 8 m, throttle centred, 3D GPS fix with ≥ 5 satellites and EKF healthy, voltage ≥ 14.4 V, within 5 m of the start point.

Safety nets in the script: if the drone would fall below 5 m while braking the hit is cut; below 3 m it rescues with full thrust (that hit counts as failed). None happened in this run.

6.3  How to read it

Spins are easy

The drone stays flat. Height lost is 0.02 to 0.17 m, drift 1 m or less. Recovery takes the longest (≈ 1.2 to 1.3 s) only because it must stop a spin.

Flips cost height

Upside-down means thrust points down. Easy flips lose about 4 to 5 m, medium about 5 m, the hard flip 8.2 m.

Bird hard is the worst case

Three hard birds lost 5.25, 9.74 and 5.68 m. The 9.74 m one reached 14.3 m from 24.1 m. The height-hold brake did its job, there was just a lot of fall to stop.

Drift is the biggest spread

Medium flips drifted 12.8, 8.7 and 12.9 m. All came back to within 0.02 to 0.12 m. Sideways room matters more than you may expect.

Plan with the worst number The worst single fall was 9.74 m and the worst drift 12.9 m. The doorman refuses hits under 8 m and cuts at 5 m, but for the first real hard hits a sensible budget is about 10 m of height above the 8 m floor, and an open field of at least 13 m radius. This is my suggestion from these thirteen hits, not a tested rule.

The pattern: AirSim loses about 1 to 2 m more height on tumbles and slides further sideways. Candidates (all hypotheses, none isolated): AirSim's motors are linear and have no inflow thrust loss, which changes how quickly it regains lift when it comes out of a tumble; the first-order motor delay is not the same as FlightGear's slew limit; the different hover value and height. The useful consequence is a good one: the PPO recovers in both, and the harsher of the two is the one to plan with.

A clean next test (not run yet): the same hit class, at least five times, from the same height in both simulators, then compare the spread, not single values.

8What this proves, and what it does not

Shown by this run

  • The whole chain works: radio → pilot → brain → AirSim → examiner → PPO → hand-back.
  • The PPO (trained on the F450, never retrained) recovers a 5-inch body in all 13 hits.
  • The doorman checks (height, throttle, radius, GPS) refuse correctly.
  • Recovery plus return works at 24 m and 40 m.

Not shown

  • Real motors: lag, voltage sag, prop inflow, damage, ESC behaviour.
  • Wind, GPS jitter, vibration, a battery under load.
  • The real motor curve (needs a thrust-stand test).
  • Loop rate 400 in AirSim (we run 200). Not tried.
  • Why AirSim loses more height than FlightGear.

Also worth knowing: the predicted hover reading (16.5) and the measured one (15) differ by ≈ 10 %, and the real drone's hover has since drifted to ≈ 0.137. There is no Gazebo Q250 in your setup, so there is no third simulator to compare with.
