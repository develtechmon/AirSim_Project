ESP32-S3 Throw Catcher

The same throw catcher as throw_real.py on the Pi (version d), running on an ESP32-S3 Super Mini: same detection, same Round 1 PPO, same 9 checks, same beeps, same 10 s hold → LAND, same take-over and the same result columns. The Pi stays your primary computer; this is the low-cost, low-power alternative.

The PPO is the driver. On the Pi it rides in a big car with a full computer (Linux, Python, onnxruntime). On the ESP32 the same driver sits in a go-kart: no operating system, no Python, so we copy its brain as plain numbers (int16) and its habits as C++ code. Same driver, same decisions, smaller car.

Why steps are written later on the ESP32: writing the flash pauses the chip for up to ≈ 50 ms. During the catch (READY → caught) the steps stay in RAM; they are written during the 10 s GUIDED hold and LAND, when ArduPilot flies the drone. The printout (console.log) is written only in WAIT. So nothing slows down the catch.

What "int16" means: each of the 102,685 numbers is stored as a whole number −32767…32767 plus one scale per row of the network (number = whole × scale). That is 200 KB instead of 411 KB, so it fits in the S3's fast internal RAM. Measured difference to the ONNX: 0.00005 rad at most (≈ 0.003°), far below sensor noise.

A4Wiring · battery OFF, props OFF

A4ESP32-S3 Super Mini on the FC's SERIAL2 (TX3 / RX3), where the Pi was4 wires · soldering iron

TX always goes to RX. The pin numbers are set at the top of throw_esp32s3.ino (FC_RX_PIN, FC_TX_PIN); any free GPIO works if 4 / 5 are taken on your board.

FC padWireESP32-S3 Super Mini pin
TX3→GPIO 4
RX3←GPIO 5
5V (the pad that powered the Pi)—5V
GND—GND
Only one computer on SERIAL2: unsolder (or unplug) the Pi from TX3 / RX3 first. Two listeners on one port garble each other's commands.
USB and battery not at the same time: on many Super Mini boards the USB 5 V is connected straight to the 5V pin. With the battery in, the FC's 5 V and the PC's 5 V would be joined. At home: battery OUT, then plug USB (the USB then powers the ESP32 and, through the 5V wire, the FC: that is fine, like plugging the FC's own USB).

B1Bench check, props off (= the Pi's --check, Throw & Fly guide C4)

B1Typed command checkprops OFF · USB in, battery OUT · after READY TO USE (outdoors)

The check needs the script past "waiting for GPS". Easiest: outdoors, power the drone from USB only (battery out, laptop on a table), wait for ESP32 READY TO USE, then type:

check
DoExpected (same as the Pi)
Wait 2 slink OK (IMU 50 Hz, ATT 50 Hz), then one line per second with PPO x.x ms
Flip CH5 OFF, then ONswitch OK, 3 short then 2 long beeps, the 9 checks (on the bench NO for armed and for the battery is right: disarmed, battery out)
CH5 ON, toss onto a pillowthrow detected (check only - motors NOT started)
Walk, shakenothing
Type stopcheck stopped, back to WAIT
The PPO x.x ms number here is the real time of one PPO step on your S3, measured in the loop. Expect ≈ 3–5 ms.

B2Props-off throw (= Throw & Fly guide C5 / C6c Step 2)

B2Same 10 steps as the Pioutdoors · battery IN · USB OUT · props OFF

CH5 OFF, CH6 middle, CH8 ALT_HOLD, stick LOW, disarmed → battery in → 1 long + 2 short → hold the drone, CH9 arm (still) → CH5 ON → 3 short → toss onto grass / a pillow → motors spin up, then it disarms by itself → CH5 OFF, CH9 to disarm → arm again without touching CH8.

Check at home (USB, battery out, type get console.log or use C1)Expected
READYREADY - THROW NOW (3 short beeps, ...)
TossThrow detected - spooling motors
After[handover] ... / [stop] ..., Throw 1 | ppo-policy | motors on ..., Saved: run0003.csv / _steps.csv / .json
Re-armmode back to ALT_HOLD - arm again with CH9 when ready

With props off it usually ends not caught in 3 s or lands at once: fine. The point is READY → motors on the toss → disarm by itself → re-arm without CH8.

B3Field throws (= Throw & Fly guide C6 and C6c)

Everything in the field is identical to the Pi: the 12 steps, the beeps, the stick LOW rule, no time limit in READY, the 10 s hold → LAND, back to ALT_HOLD, the take-over (stick middle → CH8 PosHold), CH7 for emergencies. Use the Throw & Fly guide C6 / C6c as the procedure; only the start differs: the ESP32 is ready as soon as the GPS is (no Linux boot).
