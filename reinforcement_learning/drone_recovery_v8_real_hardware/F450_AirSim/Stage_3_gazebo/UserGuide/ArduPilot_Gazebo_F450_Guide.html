STAGE 3ArduPilot + Gazebo: F450 flip-recovery test
How to run it, and every change we made to get the AirSim-trained PPO recovering in Gazebo

This guide records how the Stage 3 flip-recovery test was moved from AirSim to a second simulator (Gazebo Classic 11 with ArduPilot SITL). It covers what failed, what we measured, what we changed and why. The PPO policy was not changed. What changed: the simulated drone, its flight-controller settings, and (in a separate copy of the test folder, Stage_3_gazebo) how the knock is delivered.

One-line story. The Gazebo Iris was a different (heavier, motor-limited) drone from the one PPO learned on, so we built an AirSim-matched F450 for Gazebo. We then checked it with measurements (weight, motor PWM, flight log) and fixed three remaining differences: thrust, controller settings and prop inertia. Easy flips then recovered, but medium and hard still failed, because the way the script creates the knock made ArduPilot stumble near upside-down. With the lead hit (Change G) the knock is clean, and the AirSim-trained PPO recovered every medium and hard flip.

1. How the pieces connect

Think of it as the same pilot (PPO) and the same autopilot (ArduPilot 4.4) moved from one flight simulator to another. For the comparison to be fair, the simulated aircraft and the autopilot settings must match. Most of this guide is about making them match.

D · Flight-controller settings matched to AirSim SITL

DCopy ArduPilot's own AirSim settings into Gazebo SITLf450_airsim_match.parm

When SITL is started for AirSim, ArduPilot automatically loads Tools/autotest/default_params/airsim-quadX.parm. Gazebo SITL was running the standard defaults, so the "same autopilot" was tuned differently:

SettingGazebo beforeAirSim / nowEffect
ATC_RAT_PIT_P, _I (and RLL)0.135, 0.1350.25, 0.25About 1.9× stronger reaction to a rate demand
ATC_RAT_PIT_D (and RLL)0.00360.003About the same
ATC_RAT_*_FLTD, FLTT20 Hz50 HzLess filter delay
INS_GYRO_FILTER, INS_ACCEL_FILTER20 Hz50 HzLess filter delay
SCHED_LOOP_RATE400300Minor
MOT_THST_EXPO0.650.65 (kept)See below
Why MOT_THST_EXPO stays different from AirSim (0.0). It tells ArduPilot how thrust relates to throttle. AirSim's motors are linear, so 0.0 is right there. Gazebo's motors follow thrust ∝ speed², and the fitted value for this curve is 0.66, so 0.65 is right for Gazebo. Real motors behave like Gazebo's.

Result: peak rate rose only to 9.2–10 rad/s and the flip still stalled near upside-down. So tuning was not the main cause, but we kept it so both simulators run the same autopilot.

A heavy spinning prop resists being tipped, like a bicycle wheel. The Iris props were about 3× heavier in spin than a real F450 prop, and AirSim models no prop gyroscope at all. The change we applied (already included in setup version 2):

sed -i 's#<ixx>9.75e-06</ixx>#<ixx>2.85e-05</ixx>#; s#<iyy>0.000166704</iyy>#<iyy>2.85e-05</iyy>#; s#<izz>0.000167604</izz>#<izz>5.7e-05</izz>#' ~/ardupilot_gazebo/models/f450_airsim_base/model.sdf

grep -c "<izz>5.7e-05</izz>" ~/ardupilot_gazebo/models/f450_airsim_base/model.sdf

Result: peak rate 12.2–12.6 rad/s, hit 1.15–1.29 s, 3 / 3 recovered, lowest 11.9 / 13.2 / 16.0 m. Total inertia is now 0.0065 / 0.0078 / 0.0138 kg·m² (AirSim 0.0067 / 0.0080 / 0.0143).

But: medium and hard flips still failed or only just held (lowest 3.5–10.9 m). The recovery itself looked like AirSim's; the drone simply started it lower and falling faster, because the hit stumbled near upside-down (section 4). That led to Change G.

G · Lead hit: fixing how the knock is delivered

GKeep ArduPilot's target just ahead of the drone during the hitimpact_check.py in Stage_3_gazebo only

The script fakes the knock by asking ArduPilot to spin the drone. ArduPilot turns that into its own target attitude, the ghost (G), and chases it. We went through three versions:

A carrot on a stick in front of a donkey. Old hit: the carrot is on a cart that drives away faster than the donkey can walk; once it's more than half a lap ahead, the donkey turns round. v1: we hold a carrot 30 cm ahead, but the driver still holds his own carrot on a long stick, so the donkey only ambles. v2: the driver uses our carrot, always 30 cm ahead, so the donkey runs flat out.

In plain words: what happens during ONE hit now (the script does all of this by itself, in about 2–3 s):

Key findings for the thesis. (1) PPO trained only in AirSim recovered a drone in a different physics engine (Gazebo: quadratic motors, prop gyroscopes) in every easy, medium and hard flip tested, once the simulated drone, the autopilot settings and the way the knock is delivered matched. (2) Recovery needs spare thrust and fast motors (the Iris case), a measurable hardware requirement. (3) How an artificial hit is injected matters as much as the controller: a hit that lets the autopilot's target run away produces a worse, inconsistent start for the recovery.

4. The hit: why it stumbled ("chase the ghost") — fixed by Change G

The script creates the hit by asking ArduPilot to spin at 16 rad/s. Inside ArduPilot 4.4 this becomes a target attitude, the "ghost", that keeps spinning at 16 rad/s while the drone chases it. From the ArduPilot code:

Drone behind the ghost byArduPilot (4.4 attitude_controller_run_quat)
under 30°full "spin" command plus correction
30–60°"spin" command faded out
over 60°only steers toward the ghost, by the shortest way; past about half a turn, that can be backwards

In AirSim the flip stayed perfectly in one plane and kept spinning forward. In Gazebo, small differences (slightly slower start, sideways motion) make ArduPilot sometimes choose "backwards", which gives a 0.1–0.3 s stumble near upside-down. The log proves the mechanism, but the exact trigger isn't pinned down. That is why we call the method fragile: tiny differences decide whether it stumbles.

Status: fixed in Stage_3_gazebo by the lead hit (Change G). The old hit stays in Stage_3_v1 so the AirSim results remain reproducible.
